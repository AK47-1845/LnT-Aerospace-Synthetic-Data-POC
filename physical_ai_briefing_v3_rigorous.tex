\section{Part 2: Research Direction: What the Frontier Is Actually Working On}

\subsection{2.1 Technical Priorities Across Frontier Research Labs}
Analyzing published technical reports from leading research institutions (Physical Intelligence, Google DeepMind, NVIDIA GEAR, Stanford IRIS, UC Berkeley BAIR, Toyota Research Institute) indicates five active engineering bottlenecks:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Continuous Action Flow Matching vs. Discrete Tokenization:}
    Early Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (RT-1, RT-2) quantized continuous actions into discrete tokens, causing discretization errors and slow inference. Frontier models ($\pi_0$ from Physical Intelligence, ManiFlow) use continuous flow matching to generate high-frequency (50Hz) control trajectories directly.
    \item \textbf{Physics-Informed and 3D Spatial World Models:}
    Moving away from 2D pixel prediction toward 3D voxel/point-flow representations (e.g., PointWorld, NVIDIA Cosmos-Predict) that model physical contact, collision bounds, and spatial geometry.
    \item \textbf{Reinforced Fine-Tuning (RFT) for Dexterous Skills:}
    Imitation learning alone suffers from distributional drift when small errors accumulate. Labs are applying RL fine-tuning with stage-aware sub-goal rewards (e.g., STA-PPO) and residual policies to enable autonomous error recovery.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Embodiment Scaling Laws:}
    Empirical research (e.g., NVIDIA's EgoScale study, 2026) demonstrates log-linear scaling trends when pre-training policies on large-scale egocentric human video before adapting to specific robot morphologies.
    \item \textbf{Edge Model Compression and Real-Time Execution:}
    Compressing multi-billion parameter VLAs using channel-aware quantization (e.g., AutoQVLA) to run deterministically on embedded edge GPUs (NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{2.3 Why Pearl's Causal Ladder Explains the LLM-Robotics Failure}
Deploying standard language models directly into robotics control fails due to a fundamental representational mismatch:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rung I (Association / Observation):} Language models operate on observational correlations: $P(Y \mid X)$. They predict the most probable text token based on historical training data.
    \item \textbf{Rung II (Intervention / Action):} Physical control requires causal intervention: $P(Y \mid \text{do}(u))$. A robot must predict what happens when it applies a specific 15N torque to an object.
    \item \textbf{Rung III (Counterfactuals / Planning):} Safe execution requires evaluating alternative physical paths: $P(Y_u \mid X', Y')$.
\end{itemize}
This causal distinction justifies why industrial robotics requires dedicated world models and physics-grounded verification rather than raw prompt engineering on LLMs.

\section{Part 6: The Industrial AI \texorpdfstring{$\rightarrow$}{to} Physical AI Bridge}

\subsection{6.1 Evolution vs. Separate Technical Category}
Physical AI is fundamentally distinct from traditional Industrial AI (predictive maintenance, time-series anomaly detection):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Industrial AI (Association / Open-Loop):} Operates on 1D scalar time-series (vibration, temperature, current). Generates human notifications on minute/hour timescales.
    \item \textbf{Physical AI (Causal / Closed-Loop):} Operates on 3D spatial representations, depth point clouds, and force feedback. Directly outputs actuator commands at $\ge 50\text{Hz}$ ($\Delta t \le 20\text{ms}$).
    \item \textbf{Organizational Consequence:} Predictive maintenance software vendors cannot easily transition into robotics control; physical AI requires robotics, control systems, and mechanical engineering DNA.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{6.2 Replacement vs. Complementary Coexistence}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Where Physical AI Replaces Industrial AI:} In dynamic closed-loop manufacturing tasks (e.g., real-time CNC spindle modulation to suppress chatter vibration; real-time adaptive seam tracking in robotic welding).
    \item \textbf{Where Industrial AI Remains Dominant:} In plant-wide asset management, macro supply-chain planning, and enterprise power forecasting, where time-series models remain computationally optimal.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{6.3 The Domain Engineering Moat: Formal Kinematics \& Safety}
Deploying Physical AI in manufacturing requires rigorous domain engineering that software-only AI labs cannot provide:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Kinematic Invariants \& Singularity Management:}
    In robotic manipulation, the mapping from end-effector Cartesian velocities $\dot{x} \in \mathbb{R}^6$ to joint velocities $\dot{q} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is governed by the manipulator Jacobian matrix $J(q) \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times n}$ via $\dot{x} = J(q)\dot{q}$. Inverting this relationship to command joint actuators requires:
    \[
    \dot{q} = J^{\dagger}(q)\dot{x}
    \]
    where $J^{\dagger}$ is the Moore-Penrose pseudo-inverse. In the vicinity of kinematic singularities, the Yoshikawa manipulability measure:
    \[
    \mu(q) = \sqrt{\det\left(J(q)J^T(q)\right)} \longrightarrow 0
    \]
    Under this condition, even a small, bounded Cartesian trajectory command output by a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policy demands unbounded joint velocities ($\|\dot{q}\| \to \infty$). In real factory cells, this triggers immediate drive over-current trips, joint emergency stops, or severe mechanical gearbox damage. Pure ML engineers lacking controls domain expertise cannot build the singularity-robust inverse kinematics and damped least-squares (DLS) filtering layers required to deploy neural policies on real servo hardware.

    \item \textbf{Deterministic Timing Boundaries:}
    Synchronizing neural policy inference with real-time fieldbus cycles ($<1\text{ms}$ jitter on EtherCAT/Profinet) via RTOS interfaces.

    \item \textbf{Functional Safety Architecture:}
    Designing dual-channel safety circuits and hardware interlocks that comply with ISO 13849 (Cat 4 / PLe).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{6.4 The Closed-Loop Operational Data Flywheel}
LTTS should implement a 5-stage deployment architecture:
\begin{enumerate}
